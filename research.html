Research

Three ways I read time.

My research sits where mathematics, signal processing and deep learning meet: models for multivariate time-series that are accurate, efficient and honestly evaluated — applied to health and finance.

Research areas

Where I focus.

01

Deep Learning for Time-Series Classification

Multivariate signals are long, noisy, and messy. I model them in both the time and frequency domains — and as graphs — to classify them reliably, with top results on public benchmarks.

  • Time-domain and frequency-domain representations
  • Graph-based models of the structure between channels
  • LSTMs, convolutional blocks and attention-based fusion
  • Augmentation for small, real-world datasets

02

AI & Agentic Systems for Health

From wearable physiological signals to emotion states and eating-disorder research, I build models with clinicians and neuroscientists in the loop — and explore agentic AI that can support health researchers end-to-end.

  • Multimodal wearables: heart rate, EDA, BVP, temperature, motion
  • Personalized models fine-tuned to each participant
  • Ecological momentary assessment as ground truth
  • Toward agentic assistants for health research

03

AI in Finance

I stress-test AI against classic methods on real markets — portfolio optimization, foundation models, and LLM sentiment — with transaction costs, walk-forward splits, and statistical significance built in.

  • Leakage-free walk-forward backtesting with costs
  • Classic, evolutionary and deep-learning portfolios
  • Foundation models (Chronos-2) and LLM sentiment
  • Deflated Sharpe ratio and block-bootstrap testing

Publication

Peer-reviewed work.

Publication · IEEE · 2026

Prediction of Feelings in Individuals with Eating Disorders Using Physiological Signals

M. Hammami, H. Frigui, A. Karem, C. Ralph-Nearman

4th Cognitive Models and Artificial Intelligence Conference (AICCONF) · DOI 10.1109/AICCONF69182.2026.11600712

A 30-day longitudinal study with Empatica E4 wearables — 67+ hours, 7 physiological signals — predicting anxiety, tiredness, and fear of weight gain in individuals with atypical anorexia nervosa. A multisampling augmentation and an optimized LSTM cut error by 29%; participant-specific fine-tuning brought the weighted global MAE to 0.0989 — a step toward real-time, personalized monitoring.

  • 30 days
  • 67+ h of signals
  • 320 → 25,600 sequences
  • −29% MAE
  • MAE 0.0989
